\section{Results}
\label{sec:experiments_results}

This section reports the results of applying \gls{car} to the six environments.
Section~\ref{ssec:initial_policy_training} verifies that the initial policies reach a competent level of play, while Section~\ref{ssec:surrogate_policy_training} evaluates whether the surrogate policies faithfully reproduce their behavior using the \BehaviorReconstructionName.
Section~\ref{ssec:experiments_latent_space_analysis} analyzes the latent representations using the \AbstractionScoreName and \ClusteringUniversalityName.


\subsection{Initial policy training}
\label{ssec:initial_policy_training}

\input{figures/experiments/policy_training.tex}

Figure~\ref{fig:initial_policy_training} shows how the initial policies improve during \gls{ppo} training.
For each environment, the mean episode return is normalized so that $0\%$ and $100\%$ correspond to the lowest and highest mean return observed during that run.
In every environment the return increases and then reaches a plateau, at which point training is stopped.
The number of iterations required to reach this plateau varies from a few hundred, for Pong, to several thousand, for Surround and Ms.\ Pac-Man.
For Ms.\ Pac-Man the return decreases after its peak.
Since the best checkpoint is retained rather than the last one, this does not affect the policy that is subsequently analyzed.

To confirm that the trained policies play competently, their scores are compared with the reference scores reported by \cite{machado2017revisitingarcadelearningenvironment}.
Each initial policy reaches a score of the same order as these references, so it constitutes a reasonable target for imitation.

\subsection{Surrogate policy training}
\label{ssec:surrogate_policy_training}

Figure~\ref{fig:surrogate_policy_training} shows, for each environment, the training action loss of the three surrogate policies.
In every case the loss decreases smoothly, and the three independently trained policies follow almost identical trajectories, converging to the same value.

We further evaluate the ability of the surrogate policies to replicate the behavior of the initial policies using the \BehaviorReconstructionName (Table~\ref{tab:environments_metrics}).
The surrogate policies reach a \BehaviorReconstructionName of $0.96$ on average over 100 evaluation episodes, ranging from $0.83$ for Breakout to $1.00$ for Ms.\ Pac-Man and Surround.
They therefore reproduce the decisions of the initial policies closely enough for their latent representations to be analyzed in place of those of the initial policies.

\subsection{Latent space analysis}
\label{ssec:experiments_latent_space_analysis}

With the behavioral fidelity of the surrogate policies established, this section analyzes their latent representations using the \AbstractionScoreName and \ClusteringUniversalityName, also reported in Table~\ref{tab:environments_metrics}.

For the remainder of the analysis, a random subset of 50,000 observations is embedded into the \gls{ls} of each surrogate policy, using the representation extracted directly after the last convolutional layer, and the resulting point cloud $\textcolor{myblue}{\mathcal{E}}$ is partitioned with $k$-means clustering \cite{macqueen1967multivariate}.

\input{figures/clustering_agent_representation/clustering_curves.tex}

Figure~\ref{fig:clustering_universality_curves} reports the evolution of \ClusteringUniversalityName with respect to the number of clusters.
After the first few values of $K$, every curve settles onto a plateau whose level depends on the environment, from about $0.45$--$0.50$ for Breakout and Pong to about $0.65$ for Ms.\ Pac-Man and Space Invaders.
Since \ClusteringUniversalityName is computed from the \gls{ari}, and an \gls{ari} of $0.5$ already corresponds to substantial agreement \cite{amezquita2020orchestrating}, these values indicate that independently trained surrogate policies recover a similar cluster structure in every environment, and that this structure is stable with respect to the number of clusters.
The strength of this agreement varies across environments, but remains clearly above chance everywhere.
The absence of a single optimal number of clusters suggests that $K$ should be chosen according to the level of granularity desired for the analysis.

The \AbstractionScoreName, in contrast, varies markedly across environments.
It is close to $1$ for Pong ($0.99$) and high for Breakout, Mario Bros and Space Invaders ($0.69$ to $0.82$): in these environments the latent clusters are largely independent of a direct clustering of the observations or of the action distributions.
It is lower for Surround ($0.55$) and Ms.\ Pac-Man ($0.43$).
In these two environments the position of the agent on the screen largely determines both the raw observation and the decision to take, so a clustering of the \gls{ls} naturally coincides with a clustering of the observations, and the \AbstractionScoreName cannot separate the two; a low score does not by itself imply that the representation is not abstract.

\input{figures/clustering_agent_representation/semantic_granularity.tex}

With fewer clusters, \gls{car} highlights broad strategic patterns.
For instance, in the Mario Bros environment, the two main groups correspond to whether the POW block is present (highlighted in red in Figure~\ref{fig:semantic_granularity}A) or not.
This is particularly interesting, as the use of the POW block is a key determinant of strategy in this environment \cite{mario_bros_atari_2600}.

With a larger number of clusters, the analysis captures more fine-grained tactical behaviors, such as ``Attacking the left'' and ``Navigating through the level'' (see Figure~\ref{fig:semantic_granularity}B).

Both perspectives are valid, depending on whether the goal is to study strategic or tactical behaviors.
Similar interpretable structures can be observed across the evaluated environments.
