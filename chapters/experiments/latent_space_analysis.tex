\section{Quantitative Analysis}
\label{sec:experiments_latent_space_analysis}

This section analyzes the \gls{ls} of the surrogate policies using \gls{cu}, \gls{as}, and \gls{acu}.
To sample the \gls{ls}, a random subset of 20,000 observations is selected for each environment.
The same subset is then embedded into the \gls{ls} of each surrogate policy at each analyzed layer.
Each point cloud is partitioned using $k$-means clustering \cite{macqueen1967multivariate} for each number of clusters $K \in [2,20]$.
The three metrics are then computed from these point clouds and their corresponding clusterings.
The full results across all environments and analyzed layers are reported in Appendix~\ref{chap:appendix}.

Figure~\ref{fig:latent_space_curves_by_layer_depth} reports, for each environment, \gls{cu}, \gls{as}, and \gls{acu} averaged over $K \in [2,20]$ as a function of the depth of the analyzed layer.
Across environments, \gls{cu} tends to be highest in the earliest layers and decreases with depth, while \gls{as} follows the opposite trend and increases as the representation becomes less tied to the raw observation, the action distribution, and the network's initialization.
As \gls{acu} combines these two effects, its evolution depends on the environment.

The corresponding linear fits show that \gls{cu} decreases from $0.72$ at the first layer to $0.50$ at the last, with a slope of approximately $-0.04$ per layer, while \gls{as} increases from $0.28$ to $0.80$, with a slope of approximately $+0.07$ per layer.
Over the same range, \gls{acu} increases more moderately from $0.00$ to $0.30$, with a slope of approximately $+0.03$ per layer.
This suggests that, despite the environment-specific variations, deeper representations tend on average to provide a better trade-off between \gls{cu} and \gls{as}.

For each environment, the layer with the highest average \gls{acu} across the tested cluster numbers is selected.
Table~\ref{tab:latent_space_best_layer} reports the selected layer for each environment, together with the corresponding average \gls{cu}, \gls{as}, and \gls{acu}.

The evolution of these three metrics with the number of clusters for the selected layer is then shown in Figure~\ref{fig:latent_space_curves_by_metric}.
This provides a more detailed view of the trade-off between consistency and abstraction for each environment.

\PongName achieves the highest average \gls{acu} among the six environments, with a value of $0.45 \pm 0.07$.
This results from a moderate \gls{cu} combined with an \gls{as} that saturates from the third layer onward.
In contrast, \MarioBrosName achieves the highest \gls{as}, with $0.94 \pm 0.02$, but also the lowest \gls{cu}, with $0.37 \pm 0.08$.
The resulting \gls{acu} is therefore among the lowest across the six environments.
