\subsection{\MarioBrosName}

Two clusterings are examined for \MarioBrosName (Table~\ref{tab:qualitative_mario_bros_metrics}), both obtained at layer 3, with $K=3$ and $K=5$.
The layer being the same, this comparison isolates the effect of $K$.

With $K=3$ (Figure~\ref{fig:qualitative_mario_bros}), the partition is organized around where the action takes place: it separates the side of the screen on which Mario fights enemies, left (cluster A) or right (cluster C), and isolates the situations in which the POW block is present (cluster B), a fixed and recognizable element of the level.
With $K=5$ (Figure~\ref{fig:qualitative_mario_bros_alt}), the POW block is still isolated (cluster E), but the partition no longer separates the left from the right side of the screen.
It distinguishes instead finer situations, mostly located on the left side of the level: Mario on the top-left platform (cluster D), attacking the enemies at the top left (cluster F), and hitting enemies from below (cluster H), together with a cluster in which Mario simply moves through the level (cluster G).
Increasing $K$ from $3$ to $5$ lowers \gls{cu} ($0.84$ to $0.75$) and \gls{acu} ($0.50$ to $0.40$), while \gls{as} is unchanged ($0.66$ and $0.65$): the finer partition is less reproducible across surrogate policies, without being more abstract.
