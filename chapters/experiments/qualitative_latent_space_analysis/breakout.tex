\subsection{\BreakoutName}

Two clusterings are examined for \BreakoutName (Table~\ref{tab:qualitative_breakout_metrics}), obtained at layer 4 with $K=2$ and at layer 6b with $K=5$.
Since both the layer and $K$ differ, the comparison below is descriptive rather than a controlled study of $K$ alone.
We call high-level concepts those that relate to the progress of the game and its key events, and low-level concepts those that relate to the immediate position of objects on the screen.

At $K=2$ (Figure~\ref{fig:qualitative_breakout_alt}), the partition captures a single high-level concept: the position of the ball relative to the brick wall, either below it (cluster A) or having passed behind it (cluster B), the strategic event that lets the ball clear bricks on its own.
At $K=5$ (Figure~\ref{fig:qualitative_breakout}), the partition also isolates the ball behind the wall (cluster E) as well as the digging of a tunnel through the left side of the wall (cluster C), and it adds low-level concepts: the beginning of the episode is split according to the position of the paddle (clusters D, F, and G).
The clustering with $K=2$ is almost perfectly reproducible across surrogate policies (\gls{cu} of $0.99$, \gls{acu} of $0.87$), whereas the one with $K=5$ is less consistent (\gls{cu} of $0.79$, \gls{acu} of $0.61$) but exposes information that the former does not.
A higher \gls{acu} therefore reflects a more stable partition, not a semantically richer one.
