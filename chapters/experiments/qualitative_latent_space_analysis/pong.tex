\subsection{\PongName}

Two clusterings are examined for \PongName (Table~\ref{tab:qualitative_pong_metrics}), obtained at layer 2 with $K=2$ and at layer 3 with $K=3$.

At layer 2 (Figure~\ref{fig:qualitative_pong_alt}), the partition separates the observations according to whether the player's score has one or two digits (clusters A and B).
This relates to a high-level quantity, the progress of the game, but it is captured only through its display at the top of the screen: the partition reflects a visual feature of the observation that says nothing about the way the agent plays.
It is nonetheless perfectly reproducible and perfectly abstract (\gls{cu}, \gls{as}, and \gls{acu} of $1.00$).
At layer 3 (Figure~\ref{fig:qualitative_pong}), the partition follows instead the vertical position of the ball, at the top (cluster C), in the middle (cluster D), or at the bottom (cluster E) of the field, a low-level concept that directly determines where the agent must place its paddle.
This clustering is less consistent across surrogate policies (\gls{cu} of $0.67 \pm 0.15$) and reaches a lower \gls{acu} ($0.65$).
The clustering with the best metrics is therefore not the most informative about the behavior of the agent: \gls{acu} measures the quality of a partition, not the relevance of its semantics.
