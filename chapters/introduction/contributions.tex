\section{Contributions}
\label{sec:contributions}

To address these four research questions, this manuscript is based on three main contributions.

The first contribution has not yet been published and addresses Research Question~\ref{rq:latent_space}.
In this contribution, we make explicit the working hypothesis that implicitly underlies much of the literature on \gls{ls} analysis, which we refer to as the \ConceptHypothesisName.
We then organize existing \gls{ls} analysis methods into four families, namely probing, interpretable directions, superposition, and clustering, according to the assumptions they make about the \gls{cs}.

The second contribution addresses Research Question~\ref{rq:taxonomy}.
This contribution takes the form of a first paper \cite{alaarabiou:hal-04685607} and an extended English version \cite{alaarabiou2026taxonomy}, both of which were submitted for review and accepted for publication.
In this contribution, we first clarify what explainability means in \gls{rl} by providing a clear frame of reference.
Building on these frame, we then conduct a comprehensive analysis of the state of the art in \gls{xrl}.

The third contribution addresses Research Questions~\ref{rq:mechanisms} and~\ref{rq:evaluation}.
In the paper \cite{alaarabiou2026car}, which has been reviewed and accepted for publication, we introduce \gls{car}, an \gls{xrl} method based on the principles of \gls{me}.
By analyzing the \glspl{lr}, \gls{car} produces explanations in the form of clusters of states that are similar from the agent's own perspective.
We further show that this representation remains stable across training, indicating that the agent develops an objective representation of a given task.

This manuscript can be viewed as a synthesis of these three contributions, brought together into a single document.
