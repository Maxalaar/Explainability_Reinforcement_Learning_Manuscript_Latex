\section{Research Context}
\label{sec:research_context}

Artificial intelligence has advanced rapidly in recent years, driven largely by major developments in deep learning.
This type of approach relies on artificial neural networks to learn complex tasks directly from data.
These networks are connectionist models in which information is represented and transformed through patterns of interactions among interconnected computational units.
This approach has led to significant advances across a wide range of domains, too numerous to list here.

Among the fields that have been transformed by deep learning is reinforcement learning, which is the main subject of this manuscript.
The goal of reinforcement learning is to enable an agent to learn how to make decisions through interactions with an environment.
At each step, the agent observes the current state of the environment and selects an action.
This action changes the state of the environment and results in a reward.
The objective of the agent is then to learn a policy that maximizes the cumulative rewards obtained over time.
Neural networks provide a powerful means of representing such policies and enable agents to learn increasingly complex behaviors.
Their use has consequently made it possible to apply reinforcement learning to problems that were previously intractable to address.

When an agent learns to act in complex environments, it can develop strategies that are themselves highly complex.
Although such agents can achieve strong performance, these strategies can be difficult for an observer to understand.
Examining the neural network that models the agent's policy does not make it to produce explanations.
The information underlying a decision is distributed across many units and connections, making it imposible to trace decision-making process by simply following the flow of information through the network.
Neural networks are therefore often regarded as "black box" models.

These challenges have led to the emergence of explainable artificial intelligence.
In the specific context of reinforcement learning, this research area is referred as Explainable Reinforcement Learning.
The goal of XRL is to develop methods that produce explanations that improve our understanding of agent behavior.
Such explanations can serve several purposes, including increasing human trust and facilitating the adoption of RL agents in real-world settings, identifying errors and biases, and verifying that previously identified problems have been effectively corrected.

Several approaches have been proposed to improve the explainability of artificial intelligence systems.
This manuscript presents methods from these different approaches, while placing particular emphasis on the mechanistic approach to explainability.
In mechanistic explainability seeks to investigate the internal mechanisms underlying the agent's decision-making process.
The objective is to understand how information is represented, transformed, and propagated within the neural network.
From this perspective, explainability is not achieved despite the use of neural networks, but through them.

This perspective naturally leads to the study of the intermediate representations learned by neural networks.
These representations are referred to as latent representations.
They result from the successive transformations applied to the input information between the input and output of the network and therefore play an intermediate role in the agent's decision-making process.
Analyzing their structure can provide insights into how information relevant to decision-making is organized within the network.
It can also help identify the mechanisms through which observations are transformed into the actions selected by the agent.

The present manuscript therefore lies at the intersection of reinforcement learning, deep learning, and explainable artificial intelligence.
This intersection leads to the following research question:
\begin{quote}
\textbf{How can the analysis of the internal representations of neural networks used by agents help identify the mechanisms underlying their decision-making processes?}
\end{quote}
