A creation that surpasses its creator.

This idea has long captured the imagination and continues to resonate in contemporary society.
Centuries before modern technology, generations had already questioned whether their creations might escape the control of their creators.

One of the earliest expressions of this idea can be found in the story of the Golem.
In Hebrew tradition, the term Golem refers to an artificial creature created by humans.
The earliest accounts describe the Golem as a protective and helpful figure, reflecting the idea that humans can create an artificial being for the benefit of society.

This positive vision becomes more ambiguous in the seventeenth century, when the Golem of Prague represents a shift in this perception.
In this story, the initially small Golem gradually grows in size and strength until it can no longer be controlled.
The creation intended to protect others therefore becomes a potential threat to them.
The only remaining solution is to destroy the creature in order to protect the community.

This motif has remained remarkably influential throughout history.
An illustration of this continuity can be found in the emergence of the notion of the robot.
The word robot was popularised by the play R.U.R. (Rossum's Universal Robots), written in 1920.
In the play, a scientist creates artificial beings designed to perform labour.
Initially conceived as servants, these artificial beings eventually turn against humanity and destroy civilisation.
This story can itself be understood as an evolution of the Golem narrative.
The author describes the relationship between the two figures by stating that “the Robot is the Golem made flesh through mass production.” Despite their destructive role in the story, the word robot eventually came to designate artificial beings created to serve humans.
Since its creation, the word robot has reflected both the hopes and fears surrounding technological progress.

Today, this fear is particularly evident concerning the development of artificial intelligence.
This field of computer science seeks to develop tools capable of automating tasks traditionally associated with human intelligence.
Its rapid advances have already transformed a wide range of domains.
Far from slowing down, this development continues to expand the capabilities and autonomy of artificial systems.

In the pursuit of increasingly autonomous agents, reinforcement learning is probably the subfield of AI that most directly seeks to achieve this goal.
In this learning paradigm, an artificial agent interacts with an environment, selects actions, and receives feedback according to the outcomes of its behaviour.
Through repeated interaction, it learns a strategy for achieving the objective defined by its designer.
This approach can produce highly effective strategies that can even surpass human capabilities.
At the same time, the resulting behaviour may be difficult to predict because it is not explicitly programmed into the machine, but learned through its interactions with the environment.

Despite the difficulty of this task, understanding the behaviour of such agents is therefore the main objective of this manuscript.
From the Golem to the robot and, today, to artificial intelligence, the same question: how can we ensure that our creations remain aligned with human intentions?
