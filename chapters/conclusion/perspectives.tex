\section{Perspectives}
\label{sec:conclusion_perspectives}

The limitations discussed above open several directions for future research.
This section presents three of them.

A first direction is to automate the semantic interpretation of the clusters.
One approach is to rely on vision-language models.
Given observations from the same cluster, such models could generate a natural language description of what distinguishes it from the others.
This approach would provide a textual description for each cluster, helping identify its specific semantics and fully automate the \gls{car} method.

A second direction is to use the identified clusters to explain the strategy of the agent.
This manuscript has shown that the \gls{ls} can be used to identify meaningful concepts.
The next step is to use these concepts to describe how the agent behaves over time.
Since each cluster groups states that are similar from the agent's perspective.
The transitions observed between these clusters during episodes would then form a simplified model of the environment, as perceived by the agent.

A third direction concerns the evaluation of explanations.
The metrics proposed in this manuscript measure structural properties of the \glspl{lr}.
They are necessary, but they are not sufficient.
According to our definition, an explanation is valuable only if it improves the observer's understanding of the agent.
Measuring this improvement requires experimental protocols from disciplines such as cognitive science and psychology.
Such protocols could assess whether an explanation actually improves the observer's mental model of the agent.
This is the ultimate goal of explainability, and evaluating it would complete the answer to Research Question~\ref{rq:evaluation}.
