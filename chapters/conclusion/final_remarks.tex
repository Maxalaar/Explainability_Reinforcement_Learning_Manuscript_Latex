\section{Final Remarks}
\label{sec:conclusion_final_remarks}

To conclude, let us step back and consider this work as a whole.

This thesis has proposed explainability methods for \gls{rl} agents that rely on the latent representations learned by \glspl{nn}.
From this perspective, explainability is not achieved despite the use of \glspl{nn}, but through them.
Because these representations are what the agent actually uses to act, explanations derived from them remain grounded in the agent's own reasoning.
These representations can be seen as a language that, once deciphered, reveals how an agent perceives its environment and makes decisions.

This endeavour may also reach beyond artificial systems.
Since \glspl{nn} are partly inspired by biological intelligence, understanding how they represent the world may shed light on how we do so ourselves.
Unlike the brain, an artificial network can be observed in its entirety, probed at will, and retrained under controlled conditions, which makes it a unique experimental object for studying representation and decision-making.

The study of latent spaces remains a largely open field, one whose exploration has only just begun.
