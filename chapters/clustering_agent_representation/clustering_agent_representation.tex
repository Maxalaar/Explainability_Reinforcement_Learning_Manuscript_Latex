\chapter{Clustering Agent Representations}
\label{chap:clustering_agent_representation}

\glsresetall

As discussed in the previou chapter, current explainability methods for Reinforcement Learning still face two major limitations.
First, there is a lack of intrinsic approaches capable of explaining the internal mechanisms of deep neural policies.
Second, methods are proposed generally lack objective metrics for evaluating the quality of the explanations they produce.

To the best of our knowledge, only \cite{zahavy2017graying} have proposed exploiting the latent space learned by neural policies in order to identify the concepts used by the agent.
Its main idea is to analyze the latent representations produced by the neural network and organize them into clusters that correspond to high-level concepts.
This method falls squarely within the deep clustering approach presented in Section~\ref{sec:clustering}.

While the core idea of this paper is innovative and closely aligned with our vision of explainability in RL, this approach suffers from two important limitations.
First, the clusters are manually constructed.
Such a procedure heavily depends on the user's expertise and neither guarantees reproducibility or scales.
Second, no evaluation protocol is proposed to assess the quality of the obtained clusters.
Their relevance is essentially determined through visual inspection.

The Clustering Agent Representations (CAR) method proposed in this chapter addresses these two limitations.
It automates concept discovery by relying on unsupervised clustering algorithms to identify the concept structure of the latent space.
It introduces an objective evaluation metric based on the stability of the extracted representations across independently trained policy.

The remainder of this chapter is organized as follows.
The first section introduces the theoretical assumptions underlying the proposed CAR method, referred to as the representation convergence hypothesis.
The second section describes the CAR method itself, with a detailed account of the extraction of latent representations using surrogate policies, along with the automatic detection of concept structures via clustering.
The third section then presents the proposed evaluation metric.
