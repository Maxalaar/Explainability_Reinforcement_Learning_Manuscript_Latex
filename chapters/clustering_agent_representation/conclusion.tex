\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:clustering_agent_representation_conclusion}

The three hypotheses investigated in this manuscript, \MechanisticHypothesisName, \ConceptHypothesisName, and \RepresentationConvergenceHypothesisName, formed the conceptual basis for the development of \gls{car}.
The experiments conducted with \gls{car} provide empirical support for these hypotheses, with the high \AbstractionScoreName and stable \ClusteringUniversalityName obtained across all environments.
These findings suggest that this hypothesis-driven approach provides a promising direction for understanding the structure of learned representations in \gls{rl} agents.

Beyond its role as an experimental framework, \gls{car} provides a general and automated method for analysing agent representations.
Both the analysis procedure and the metrics are fully automated, requiring no manual intervention or environment-specific adjustments.
This makes \gls{car} a general method that can be applied to a wide range of \gls{rl} settings.

However, several implementation choices were made for have not yet been evaluated.
These include the \gls{nn} architectures, the \gls{nn} layer selected for analysis, the distance used to compare embeddings, and the clustering algorithm.
Their impact should therefore be studied jointly to determine how changes in the analysis procedure affect the discovered \gls{cs}.

A more fundamental limitation concerns the semantic interpretation of the clusters.
While \gls{car} provides a quantitative description of the structure present in the agent's representations, the semantics assigned to individual clusters currently rely on manual interpretation.
The labels associated with the clusters in Figure~\ref{fig:semantic_granularity} represent our interpretation of the discovered structure.
This process is time-consuming and subjective.
Developing methods to automatically extract the semantics of each cluster is therefore an important direction for future work.

More broadly, when considering \gls{car} within the field of explainability, it is important to emphasize that substantial work remains to be done.
The results presented in this chapter show that \gls{car} can provide information about the concepts represented in the agent's \gls{ls}, which can therefore be considered an explanation in the sense defined in Section~\ref{sec:explanation}.
How these explanations can be used to support validation, diagnosis, or certification remains to be established \ref{explainability_reinforcement_learning}.

These limitations directly motivate several avenues for future research.
Studying the impact of the selected \gls{ls} layer, distance measures, and clustering algorithms would clarify how much the discovered structure depends on these methodological choices.
Automating cluster interpretation and developing human-centered evaluation protocols would strengthen semantic validity of the resulting explanations.
Finally, connecting \gls{car} explanations more directly to the agent's decisions and failures would help determine how they can support the broader objectives of explainability.
