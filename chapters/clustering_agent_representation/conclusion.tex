\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:clustering_agent_representation_conclusion}

The \gls{car} method implements the three hypotheses developed in this manuscript, the \MechanisticHypothesisName, the \ConceptHypothesisName, and the \RepresentationConvergenceHypothesisName.
It assumes that neural policies can be explained through their internal mechanisms, that these mechanisms are organized as concepts in the \gls{ls}, and that independently trained policies converge toward an equivalent conceptual organization.
The \ClusteringUniversalityName and \AbstractionScoreName metrics turn these assumptions into measurable quantities, which makes the hypotheses empirically testable.
The experimental study reported in Chapter~\ref{chap:experiments} provides support for this hypothesis-driven approach.

Beyond its role in testing these hypotheses, \gls{car} provides a general and automated method for analysing agent representations.
Both the analysis procedure and the metrics are fully automated, requiring no manual intervention or environment-specific adjustments.
This makes \gls{car} applicable to a wide range of \gls{rl} settings.
