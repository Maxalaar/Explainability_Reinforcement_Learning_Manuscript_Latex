\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:clustering_agent_representation_discussion}

\input{figures/clustering_agent_representation/semantic_granularity.tex}

Across the five Atari environments, our results demonstrate strong stability, as illustrated in Figure~\ref{fig:clustering_universality_curves}.
This stability indicates that there is no single optimal number of clusters to explain an agent's policy.
Instead, the level of abstraction depends on the desired granularity.
With fewer clusters, \gls{car} highlights broad strategic patterns.
For instance, in the Mario Bros environment, the two main groups correspond to whether the POW block is present (highlighted in red in Figure~\ref{fig:semantic_granularity}A) or not.
This is particularly interesting, as the use of the POW block is a key determinant of strategy in this environment \cite{mario_bros_atari_2600}.
Conversely, with a larger number of clusters, the analysis captures more fine-grained tactical behaviors, such as ``Attacking the left.'' versus ``Navigating through the level.'' (see Figure~\ref{fig:semantic_granularity}B).
Both perspectives are valid, depending on whether one seeks strategic or tactical insights.
Similar interpretable structures can be observed in all evaluated environments.

A major strength of \gls{car} lies in its general applicability.
We validated it across multiple Atari environments, and it remained effective regardless of the environment considered.
This suggests that clustering the \gls{ls} is a robust tool for extracting meaningful information about agent behavior.

As emphasized throughout this manuscript, this work aligns with the perspective of mechanistic interpretability, in which \gls{nn} are not treated as black boxes but as structured systems whose \gls{ls} can be studied and understood \cite{bereska2024mechanisticinterpretabilityaisafety}.
Research teams at Anthropic \cite{elhage2022superposition, bricken2023monosemanticity, templeton2024scaling} were among the first to propose and exploit this mechanistic approach to explainability through the use of \gls{sae}.
However, they rely on the \SuperpositionHypothesisName, unlike \gls{car}, which demonstrates that meaningful structures can be uncovered through clustering without assuming a linearly organized \gls{cs}.
